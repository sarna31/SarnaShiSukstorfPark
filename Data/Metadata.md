# Dataset Metadata

## Summary

These datasets were downloaded and prepared for use in our Electricity
Access project as part of the Environmental Data Exploration course.

The datasets contain responses to a survey recording household access to
electricity in countries noted to fall behind on electricity access by
the International Energy Agency (IEA). The goal of the survey work is to
obtain a more detailed analysis of how much electricity access
households have in lieu of a binary analysis of which households do or
do not have access to electricity.

## Database Information

Data was collected by the World Bank Group across a number of focus
countries, with data collection years spanning from 2016 to 2026. Our
current datasets include responses for:

- Cambodia
- Ethiopia
- Honduras
- Kenya
- Liberia
- Myanmar
- Nepal
- Niger
- Nigeria
- Rwanda
- Zambia

The surveys sampled from a mixture of urban and rural households across
each of the surveyed countries, spread throughout smaller states and
regions using available Census data.

Datasets are openly available on the World Bank Group’s data library,
included alongside reports and sample pdfs of the MTF surveys. Datasets
can be found on the following:

(<https://energydata.info/organization/world-bank-grou>)
(<https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/?page=1&sort_by=title&sort_order=asc&ps=15>)

## Expected Variables

Survey questions are divided into categories to identify household
information, household use of electricity, household access to sources
of electricity, and willingness to pay for electricity access.

Survey variables remain slightly inconsistent from area to area, with
certain surveys omitting certain sections or combining multiple sections
together. Our final analysis aims to merge all matching sections across
multiple surveys into one cohesive set of variables for analysis. In the
interim, we have pulled the subject list from a 2017 study in Nepal.

A. Household Roster B. Household Characteristics C. Supply and Demand of
Electricity D. Willingness to Pay for a Grid Connection E. Willingness
to Pay for Improved Electricity Supply F. Willingness to Pay for Solar
Device G. Kerosene/Fuel-Based/Candle Lighting H. Dry-Cell Batteries I.
Household Fuel Consumption for Cooking J. Use of Cooking Solutions K.
Space and Water Heating L. Willingness to Pay for an Improved Cookstove
N. Household Assets P. Household Economic Shocks Q. Street Lighting R.
Time Use S. Health Impacts T. Attitudes U. Women’s Empowerment M.
Consumption/Expenditure O. Household Land Ownership and Other Assets

For the purposes of our analysis, we are interested in the level of
electricity access households have, the means by which they obtain that
electricity access, and the various applications they can use
electricity for. This will include some of the following variables:

‘C\_2’ – is the household connected to the national grid? ‘C\_29’ – How
many hours of electricity are available each day and night from the
grid? ‘C\_64’ – In the last month how much electricity did your
household use? ‘C\_65’ – In a typical month how much did you spend on
electricity? ‘I\_3’ – In the last 12 months, did your household use
LPG/Cooking Gas/Charcoal /Kerosene/Animal Waste/Plant
Biomass/Sawdust/Biomass Briquettes/Wood Chips/ Biogas? ‘I\_13’ – In the
last 7 days, how much of this fuel source did your household use? ‘K\_1’
– Do you heat water for washing (either for washing dishes and clothes
or for bathing?) ‘K\_4’ – Do you heat your house?
